Eschés Glossary

Welcome to the Eschés glossary. Here you can find definitions and explanations of various terms related to the game.

Glossary Terms

  • Pieces

    Lower Pieces

    Occupying the forward rank(s) of an army's starting formation, these pieces are typically the first line of defense and offense. Upon reaching the opposite end of the board, they can be promoted to higher pieces.

    • Pawn: The most basic piece, moves forward one square but captures diagonally.

      On its initial move, a pawn can advance two squares.
    • Squire: Can move one square in front of itself either diagonally or vertically, and can capture pieces on those squares.

      Squires are promoted on the 2nd to last rank instead, but cannot be promoted to Queens or higher.
    • Lancer: Moves any number of squares forward in a straight line, but cannot move backward or sideways.
    • Steward: Moves one square in any direction, but cannot capture pieces.
    • Soldier: Moves like a pawn, but can only capture pieces directly in front of it.

      At any point after being captured, a turn can be used to "recruit" it back onto the board on its starting square if it is free.
    • Ferz: Moves one square diagonally in any direction.

    Higher Pieces

    These pieces are more powerful and versatile, often combining the movement abilities of lower pieces. These contain more specialized movement patterns and abilities.

    • Rook: Moves any number of squares along a row or column.
    • Knight: Moves in an "L" shape: two squares in one direction and then one square perpendicular.

      Knights can jump over other pieces.
    • Bishop: Moves diagonally any number of squares.
    • Camel: Moves like a knight, except it moves three squares in one direction and then one square perpendicular.
    • Tower: Moves one square diagonally and then any number of squares horizontally or vertically away from its starting position.
    • Cannon: Moves like a rook, but can only capture pieces by jumping over another piece; this includes friendly pieces.

    Royal Pieces

    These pieces are the most powerful and critical to the game's outcome. Losing a royal piece, without a quick recovery, can lead to a significant disadvantage. Among them is the King whose capture ends the game for an army.

    • King: The most important piece, moves one square in any direction.

      On its initial move, provided no other pieces are blocking its path and either rook has also not yet moved, the king can castle with either rook.

      The king can be placed in check by other pieces; normally, the game ends if the king is checkmated.
    • Queen: Combines the power of the rook and bishop, moving any number of squares in any direction.
    • Empress: Combines the power of the queen and knight, moving any number of squares in any direction or in an "L" shape.

      Can jump over other pieces but only when following the "L" path.
    • Princeling: Combines the power of the bishop and knight, moving any number of squares diagonally or in an "L" shape.

      Can jump over other pieces but only when following the "L" path.
