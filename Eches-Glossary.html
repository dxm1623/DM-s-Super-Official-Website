Eschés Glossary

Welcome to the Eschés glossary. Here you can find definitions and explanations of various terms related to the game.

Glossary Terms

Pieces

Lower Pieces

Occupying the forward rank(s) of an army's starting formation, these pieces are typically the first line of defense and offense. Upon reaching the opposite end of the board, they can be promoted to higher pieces.

  • Pawn:

    The most basic piece, moves forward one square but captures diagonally. On its initial move, a pawn can advance two squares.

  • Squire:

    A faithful companion to the Knight, the Squire moves and captures one square forward orthogonally. Squires are promoted on the 3rd to last rank, but cannot be promoted to a Royal piece.

  • Lancer:

    Bold and a little reckless, the Lancer charges forward and only forward. It will move any number of squares in a straight line, but can only capture pieces that are directly in its path and cannot move sideways or backward.

  • Steward:

    Moves one square in any direction, but cannot capture pieces.

  • Soldier:

    Moves like a pawn, but can only capture pieces directly in front of it. At any point after being captured, a turn can be used to "recruit" it back onto the board if its starting square is free.

  • Ferz:

    Moves one square diagonally in any direction.

Middle Pieces

These pieces are more powerful and versatile, often combining the movement abilities of lower pieces.

  • Rook:

    Moves any number of squares along a row or column. Provided it is both its first move and the king's first move, the rook and king can castle together.

  • Knight:

    Moves in an "L" shape: two squares in one direction and then one square perpendicular. Knights can jump over other pieces.

  • Bishop:

    Seeking to spread the good word of .

  • Camel:

    This stubborn steed moves like a knight, except it advances three squares in one direction and then one square perpendicular.

  • Tower:

    The imposing tower moves any number of squares along a row or column like a rook, or one square diagonally. It is also capable of castling with the king.

  • Cannon:

    A hefty cannon can be moved like a rook, but it can only capture pieces by jumping over another piece, although it doesn't necessarily need to jump over an opponent's piece to make a capture.

  • Jester:

    This trickster moves once in any direction, with a killing joke: rather than capturing a piece, it will swap places with it.

  • Unicorn:

    The mythical steed gallops like that of a knight, but for an unlimited number of steps in any direction in a chain of "L" jumps.

  • Chamberlain:

    The resident treasurer and housesitter for the royal family, the Chamberlain moves two spaces either horizontally or vertically, but captures pieces diagonally. If it is on the same row as another Middle or higher piece, it can swap places with it as long as it is not blocked by other pieces.

High Pieces

These pieces are the most powerful and critical to the game. Losing one can mean a significant disadvantage.

  • Queen:

    Combines the power of the rook and bishop, moving any number of squares in any direction.

  • Empress:

    Combines the power of the queen and knight, moving any number of squares in any direction or in an "L" shape. It can jump over pieces when moving in an "L" shape.

  • Princeling:

    Combines the power of the bishop and knight, moving any number of squares diagonally or in an "L" shape. It can jump over pieces while following the "L" pattern.

Royal Pieces

The most crucial pieces in the game, whose capture typically results in the end of the game. As hedgemons over their armies, they are subject to check and checkmate.

  • King:

    Moves one square in any direction. On its initial move (with a rook that hasn’t moved yet and no pieces blocking its path), the king can castle. The king can be placed in check; normally, checkmate means the game ends.

  • Archduke:

    Moves one square in any direction, but can also move two squares diagonally. The Archduke can jump over pieces when moving two squares diagonally.

Miscellaneous

Oddballs and misfits of the world, who rarely hold allegiances yet often play crucial roles in the game.

  • Duck:

    Owing to a clerical error long ago, this elegant yet territorial swan was misclassified as a duck. Rather than an actual duck, this one is annoying and feckless. It can only be moved at the end of every player's turn
