Case Study: Modeling With Maya, Substance Painter, & Unity

Type: Solo Design & Development | Duration: 3 Months

Product: Unity Render With 3 Modelled Assets With Environment,and Narrative Worldbuilding

    During my final semester of my undergraduate studies, I partaked in an extensive project that involved crafting a 3D scene from scratch, utilizing a combination of Maya for modeling, Substance Painter for texturing, and Unity for scene assembly and rendering. The project was structured around the creation of a cohesive scene that, by the end of my ideation process, would included a journal as the "hero" asset, and a pencil and table as supporting elements.

    Prior to my work on this project, I had some experience already in 3D modeling and texturing but only with low-cost or open-source software such as Blender. The main modeling software utilized for this particular assignment was Maya, which is a professional 3D modeling and animation software widely used in the industry with a wider range of features and capabilities. However, while the almost dizzying array of tools and options in Maya was initially overwhelming for me, it still retained much of the core functionality that I was familiar with from Blender which I was able to carry over.

    After settling on a narrative concept--that being an existing narrative that I had previously worked on that I wished to expand upon--I began by first gathering reference images for the "hero" asset, the main asset of the scene, which I envisioned as a somewhat worn, leather-bound journal. I scoured the internet for images that captured the essence of the journal I had in mind, paying close attention to details such as texture, color, and wear--ending up with the visual of a leather-bound journal with slightly yellowed pages and a brass buckle to secure it.

    With a clear vision in mind, I began the modeling process in Maya. I started with the journal, creating the basic shapes for the cover and the pages (which I modelled as being opened halfway) primarily through polygon modeling. I then duplicated the cover to create the back cover, which in turn would support the buckle mechanism (which I primarily referenced belts in designing the shape of the buckle itself and the strap).

    Once the model itself was complete, I moved on to the texturing phase. This involved unwrapping the UVs of the journal model to create a 2D representation of the 3D surface. I then imported the UV layout into Substance Painter, where I began the process of painting the textures directly onto the model. I focused on achieving a realistic leather look for the cover and a more worn appearance for the pages, complete with subtle variations in color and texture to simulate wear and tear. The buckle itself would be distinct, featuring a polished brass finish that contrasted with the matte texture of the leather.

    I would repeat this process with my two supporting assets, the pencil and the table. For the pencil, I aimed for a classic wooden look with realistic graphite shading, while the table would have a more polished appearance, showcasing the wood grain and any imperfections to enhance realism. The table was the simplest of the assets, requiring less intricate modeling and texturing compared to the journal and pencil, but I did use the opportunity to explore more efficient UV mapping techniques to streamline the process--primarily by exploiting overlapping UVs for the table legs, which were identical in shape and texture. When creating the pencil, I experimented with dividing the model itself while creating the mesh so that I could more precisely control the texturing of different parts of the pencil, namely the tip of the pencil where I wanted to achieve a sharper, more defined look where the yellow paint met the shaved wood, and the wood met the graphite.

    Once the initial test renders were complete, I exported the models into Unity, where I set up the scene and began integrating the assets. This involved creating the environment, placing the journal, pencil, and table within the scene, and adjusting the lighting to achieve the desired mood. The other assets in the scene were primarily decorative elements, such as the room template and the trees in the background, which I sourced from Unity's asset store to enhance the overall composition. After adjusting the main camera position, I rendered the final scene from two distinct angles to showcase the assets effectively.
